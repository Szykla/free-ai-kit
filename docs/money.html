Where your AI learning money is well spent

Most of what paid AI courses teach is free somewhere. A few paid offers do add something real. Here's who they are, who they're for, and a checklist you can use on any offer yourself.

Start here

Finish the free courses first

If you're new, don't pay anyone yet. These are free, made by the people who build the tools, and cover more than most paid beginner courses.

Judge any offer yourself

The 9-point checklist

Give each point 0, 1 or 2. It takes ten minutes on the sales page and a quick search for reviews.

  1. Free proof firstDo they give real work away (videos, code, guides) that you can judge before paying?
  2. What the money addsCan they say what you get beyond their free content: feedback, office hours, templates, a group?
  3. Honest about fitDo they say who should not buy? Good teachers do.
  4. No income promises"$10K a month", earnings screenshots and "$20K clients" headlines lose points. A skill is fine to sell; an outcome isn't.
  5. Refunds and cancellingIs there a clear refund window and an easy cancel? "All sales final" counts against it. A refund for finishing the work is a strong plus.
  6. Long-term membersLook for real people saying they're "still paying after 6+ months", not just the star average.
  7. Referral moneyIf members or review sites earn commission for sending you, discount their praise.
  8. The upsell ladderA cheap entry that leads to a $2,000+ "inner circle"? Judge the top step, since that's the real product.
  9. Free alternativesDoes the toolmaker teach the same thing free? Then the paid offer has to add people, not content.
14–18: worth it for its audience 9–13: only if you're exactly who it's for under 9: keep your money

Walk away from anything that sells "AI income" itself: AI influencer models, faceless channels, or accelerators that lead with earnings screenshots. You're paying for the dream, not the skill.
