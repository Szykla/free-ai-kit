# Make the AI your tutor, not your answer key

Checked: November 2026 · Works on: ChatGPT Free (main path), Claude Free, Gemini with a personal Google Account, or any free chat AI with the prompt in step 2 · Time: 25 min

You need: one thing you want to understand, not just get done. Pick something you have tried to learn before and gave up on.

Plan notes: ChatGPT's study mode is on every plan, including Free. Gemini's Guided Learning needs you to be signed in. Gemini's extra study tools (quizzes, flashcards) are for users 18 and over, not on work or school accounts. Claude's Learning option is moving from the styles menu to a skill, so where you find it depends on your account.

Video version: [video script](video-script.md) (video coming later) · Next lesson: this is the newest lesson. Go back to [the start](../01-first-useful-conversation/README.md) when you want a refresher.

## What we'll make

In this tutorial we will make a short learning session where the AI asks the questions and you do the explaining. Maria wants to understand her bakery's food cost: what share of a croissant's price goes on flour and butter, and why it matters. Ask an assistant normally and it hands her the formula, and she forgets it by Friday. This time it will make her work it out. By the end she has a one-line rule written in her own words, and she has checked it herself.

## Why this works

When you only read an answer, it feels like you understood it, but most of it is gone in a day. When you explain something in your own words and get stuck, you find the exact gap. The AI then fills that gap and nothing else. Teachers call that uncomfortable stuck moment the zone of frustration. It is where the learning happens, so don't let the AI rescue you from it too early.

## Two words to know first

- **Study mode**: a setting in some assistants that makes them ask guiding questions instead of giving the answer straight away.
- **Explain it back**: you say in your own words what you just learned, and the AI checks what you got right and what you missed.

## Steps

1. **New chat: turn on the tutor setting, if your app has one.** In ChatGPT on the web, type `@study` in the message box, or select **+** and search for "study". You can also go straight to chatgpt.com/studymode.
   > Check: the message box shows that study mode is on.
   > No study mode in your app? Skip to step 2. The prompt there works in any chat AI.

2. **Message box: tell it how to teach you.** Paste this, with your own topic in place of Maria's:

   *"I want to understand food cost for my bakery: what share of a product's price goes on ingredients, and why it matters. Don't give me the answer. Ask me one question at a time. Wait for my reply. If I'm wrong, give me a hint, not the fix. After every three questions, ask me to explain what I've learned so far in my own words. I already know my ingredient prices. I don't know any business formulas."*
   > Check: the reply ends with a question for you, not with a formula or a long explanation.
   > The last two sentences matter most. Saying what you already know lets it start at your level.

   > Example reply. Yours will be worded differently.
   > "Let's start with one croissant. Roughly how much do you spend on the flour, butter and other ingredients that go into it?"

3. **Chat: answer in your own words, even if you're unsure.** Maria types: *"Maybe 60 cents? Butter is the expensive part."* Answer every question before you read on. Write "I don't know" if you don't. That is a real answer, and it tells the tutor where to go next.
   > Check: the next message builds on your answer. It should not ignore it or jump to the answer.

4. **Chat: stay in the stuck moment once.** When a question stumps you, ask for a hint, not the answer: *"Give me a smaller hint."* Ask up to twice before you let it show you.
   > Check: you get a nudge (a smaller question or an example) and you take the next step yourself.

5. **Chat: explain it back.** After a few rounds, the tutor will ask you to sum up. If it doesn't, ask: *"Let me explain it back. Tell me what I got right, what I missed, and what I got wrong."* Then write your explanation in two or three sentences.
   > Check: the reply names at least one thing you got right and one thing to fix or add.

6. **Chat: test it on a new case.** Type: *"Give me one new example to try on my own, and don't solve it."* Maria gets a cinnamon roll with different prices, works out its food cost, and sends her answer.
   > Check: you solved the new example without asking for the method again. If you couldn't, go back to step 4 on the part that tripped you up.

7. **Notes app: write the one-line rule yourself.** Close the chat window first, then write what you learned in one line. Maria writes: *"Food cost = ingredient cost ÷ selling price. Mine should stay under about a third, or butter price rises eat my profit."* Then check that line against one trusted source, like a textbook or your accountant.
   > Check: you wrote the rule without looking, and a source you trust agrees with it.
   > The AI can be wrong while sounding sure. Lesson 5, *Check before you trust*, shows how to check it.

## If it went wrong

- It gives you the whole answer anyway -> type: *"Stop. You gave me the answer. Ask me a question instead."* -> if it keeps happening, start a new chat and paste the step 2 prompt as your first message.
- The questions are too easy or too hard -> say what you already know, for example *"I know percentages, I don't know business terms"* -> it should adjust its next question.
- You get stuck for good -> ask for the answer, then ask for a new example straight away (step 6). Seeing the answer once is fine. Never trying it yourself is the problem.
- It praises everything you say -> type: *"Be strict. Point out what's wrong or missing in my last answer."*
- Still stuck? Paste this into the assistant: "I'm on step {n} of a tutorial about learning with an AI tutor and I see {x}. What should I check?"

## Stay safe

For homework, exams or graded work, follow your school's or teacher's rules on AI first. Don't paste anything private into the chat, like real customer names, a payslip or your bank details. Use made-up or rounded numbers, the way Maria does. Check anything about health, law or money with a real expert before you act on it.

## Practice on your own life

Start: pick one thing you have tried to learn and dropped, like reading a payslip, a rule in a sport you watch, or what your phone contract really costs. Goal: one 15-minute session where you answer every question first, explain it back once, and solve one new example on your own. Finish with a one-line rule in your notes app, written without looking.

## What you made

You turned an answer machine into a tutor that makes you think. You also have a short prompt you can reuse for any topic. Use it whenever you notice you're copying an answer you don't really understand.
